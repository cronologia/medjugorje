Medjugorje — Chronology

Chronology of the reported Marian apparitions at Medjugorje, Bosnia and Herzegovina — the reports since 1981, the Church authorities' acts about them, and the 2024 nihil obstat that expressly declares nothing about their supernatural character

An open, source-referenced chronology of the reported apparitions of the Virgin Mary at Medjugorje (parish of St. James, Diocese of Mostar-Duvno, Bosnia and Herzegovina) from June 1981 to the present, and of every located Church act about them — from the diocesan commissions and the 1991 Zadar Declaration to the 2024 nihil obstat 'The Queen of Peace'.

How far the case went

Each rung is one authority's act about one object, at one date, citing the document — and the objects are never merged. Three recur here and must be kept apart: the supernatural character of the reported apparitions and messages, which no authority has ever affirmed and which the 2024 Norms say will, as a rule, never be declared; worship, devotion and pilgrimage at Medjugorje, which is what the pastoral acts of 2017–2024 regulate and encourage; and named persons, whose judgments are their own rungs. The operative judgment on the first object was, from 1991 to 2024, the Zadar Declaration's 'it can not be affirmed' — and the 2024 nihil obstat, the most favourable act in the file, says in its own text that it 'does not imply a declaration of the supernatural character of the phenomenon'. Informal spoken statements — Bishop Žanić's 1981 'the children are not lying', Pope Francis's 2017 in-flight remarks — appear in the chronology but not on this ladder, because they are not acts of judgment. Several of the acts below survive only on labeled document mirrors; the rung text says which.

Step by step, with the documents

Diocese of Mostar-Duvno — the commissions on the apparitions 1982–1986

✗Investigated — concluded against

Bishop Pavao Žanić of Mostar-Duvno: a four-member diocesan commission from 1982, enlarged to fifteen members in 1984.

Bishop Žanić — who in 1981 had publicly defended the children's sincerity ('I am convinced: the children are not lying!', sermon of 25 July 1981, per the shrine's own site) — established a commission of inquiry in 1982 and enlarged it in 1984. Its final vote, in 1986, is known only through the bishop's own 1990 published account: '11 of the members said that there is nothing supernatural in the events of Medjugorje, 2 (franciscans) claimed that the apparitions are authentic, 1 member said that there was something "in nucleo" (in the beginning) and 1 abstained.' The commission's report itself was never published. The commonly cited dates — establishment 11 January 1982, final vote 2 May 1986 — were not verified against any document for this dataset. The bishop's 1981-to-1984 reversal has two competing explanations in the literature: sympathetic sources say secret-police pressure, critical sources point to the Ivica Vego affair and the taped early interviews. Both are perspectives, recorded here as such.

Kuharić–Žanić communiqué — the case leaves the diocese; pilgrimages motivated by the supernatural claim barred 9 January 1987

—Investigated — no verdict issued

Cardinal Franjo Kuharić of Zagreb and Bishop Pavao Žanić, jointly, at the suggestion of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith; published in Glas Koncila, 18 January 1987.

The joint communiqué transferred the judgment of the case from the diocesan commission to a commission of the Yugoslav Bishops' Conference, with interim discipline: 'it is not permitted either to organize pilgrimages or to arrange other manifestations motivated by the supernatural character which might be attributed to the Medjugorje events.' Two objects in one act: jurisdiction over the judgment moved up, and worship motivated by the unjudged claim was restricted in the meantime. No primary document is online; the text is cited from labeled mirrors.

[17]

Bp. Žanić, 'The Truth About Medjugorje' — a published position, not a decree 1990

✗Investigated — concluded against

Bishop Pavao Žanić of Mostar-Duvno, after jurisdiction over the judgment had passed to the Bishops' Conference commission in 1987.

The bishop's published statement is unambiguous about his own conclusion: 'I have at least 20 reasons not to believe… the apparitions are not of the supernatural', and 'It is a terrible sin to attribute one's own lies to Our Lady.' It is quoted here as what it is — the ordinary's published personal position, issued when the canonical judgment no longer lay with him — and not as a decree of the diocese. The text survives on a labeled document-mirror blog; a second mirror returned 503 when checked for this dataset.

Bishops' Conference of Yugoslavia — the Zadar Declaration on the apparitions 10 April 1991

—Investigated — no verdict issued

The Bishops' Conference of Yugoslavia, meeting at Zadar; the vote is reported as 19 in favour with 1 abstention — a figure from secondary accounts, not the declaration text.

The operative sentence: 'On the basis of the investigations so far, it can not be affirmed that one is dealing with supernatural apparitions and revelations' — the classic non constat de supernaturalitate, which leaves the question open in both directions. The same declaration mandated pastoral care: gatherings of the faithful at Medjugorje 'require the pastoral attention and care, first of all, of the local Bishop… so that… a healthy devotion towards the Blessed Virgin Mary according to the teachings of the Church may be promoted.' This remained the ecclesial judgment the CDF itself cited for more than three decades, until 2024. No primary conference archive is online; the text is cited from labeled mirrors.

Bp. Perić — 'constat de non supernaturalitate', his stated conviction 1997–2017

✗Investigated — concluded against

Bishop Ratko Perić of Mostar-Duvno (1993–2020), Žanić's successor.

Perić went further than Zadar and said so in writing: 'my conviction and position is not only non constat de supernaturalitate but also the other formula constat de non supernaturalitate' (letter to Thierry Boutet of Famille Chrétienne, 2 October 1997). On 26 February 2017, on the diocesan site, he stated of the first seven days of reports: 'the Madonna has not appeared in Medjugorje! This is the truth that we sustain…', presenting it as 'the position of this Curia throughout this period'. The weight of these statements is itself the standing dispute: the CDF wrote in 1998 that the Mostar bishop's declaration 'should be considered the expression of the personal conviction of the Bishop of Mostar which he has the right to express as Ordinary of the place, but which is and remains his personal opinion', while Perić's 2017 text presents it as the Curia's position. Both characterizations are documented; this page records both and resolves neither. The diocesan site was unreachable from this session; the 2017 quote is carried via Catholic press.

CDF (Abp. Bertone) — the standing judgment confirmed; private pilgrimages 26 May 1998

—Investigated — no verdict issued

Archbishop Tarcisio Bertone, Secretary of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith, letter to Bishop Gilbert Aubry of Saint-Denis-de-la-Réunion (Prot. 154/81-06419).

Three points, each about a distinct object. On the apparitions' credibility, the CDF deferred to Zadar 1991, quoting its non-constat sentence and noting that competence now lay with the Bishops' Conference of Bosnia-Herzegovina. On Bishop Perić's harder formula, it ruled that his declaration 'is and remains his personal opinion'. And on worship: private pilgrimages 'are permitted on condition that they are not regarded as an authentication of events still taking place and which still call for an examination by the Church.' The letter is not on vatican.va; it is cited from labeled mirrors.

Rome — the person of Fr. Tomislav Vlašić (not the apparitions) 2008–2020

✗Investigated — concluded against

The Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith (Cardinal Levada; decree published through the Diocese of Mostar-Duvno), Pope Benedict XVI, and the CDF again in 2020.

This rung judges a man, not the events, and is kept separate for exactly that reason. The CDF's decree of 25 January 2008 (Prot. 144/1985) against Fr. Tomislav Vlašić OFM — widely described as the seers' early 'spiritual director', a label the Mostar chancery and critics dispute — cites 'diffusion of dubious doctrine, manipulation of consciences, suspected mysticism, disobedience towards legitimately issued orders', acts contra sextum and 'suspicion of heresy and schism', imposes assigned residence and prohibits contact with his 'Kraljice Mira' community. In March 2009, at his own petition, Benedict XVI laicized him, dismissed him from the Franciscans and remitted the censure (made public 24 July 2009). On 15 July 2020 a CDF decree declared him excommunicated for continuing to pose as a priest and simulate sacraments; the decree itself was not directly reached and is cited from the Diocese of Brescia's announcement as reported. Nothing in any of these acts adjudicates the apparitions.

The Ruini commission — findings on the apparitions delivered, never published 2010–2014; votes leaked 2017

?A ruling is reported; no document located

The international commission of inquiry on Medjugorje constituted at the CDF under Cardinal Camillo Ruini by Benedict XVI, announced 17 March 2010.

The commission's existence, chairmanship, reserved procedure and delivery are documented: the 2010 Holy See communiqué (which names no members — the reported composition is press reporting) states it would work 'in maniera riservata', submitting its findings to the Congregation; the final meeting and delivery of 17–18 January 2014 were announced to journalists by the press office. The findings themselves have never been published. What circulates — 13 votes in favour, 1 against, 1 suspensive on the supernatural character of the first seven appearances (24 June–3 July 1981), and a majority of suspensive votes with 'many doubts' on the phenomenon from late 1981 onward — is Andrea Tornielli's leak of 16 May 2017, never confirmed by publication, partially echoed by Pope Francis's own in-flight remarks three days earlier and by the 2024 Note's reference to divergent prior studies. Every vote figure on this rung is the leak's.

The report has never been published, and no press bulletin exists even for its delivery: press.vatican.va was checked for 17 and 18 January 2014 and carries no item — the record is the press office's statements to journalists as carried by Catholic media. The commission's membership beyond Ruini likewise rests on press reporting, not on the 2010 communiqué.

CDF via Nuncio Viganò — events presuming the apparitions' credibility (a discipline, not a verdict) 21 October 2013

—Investigated — no verdict issued

Nuncio Carlo Maria Viganò, writing to the USCCB general secretary at the request of CDF Prefect Gerhard Müller.

Pending the CDF's evaluation of the case, 'clerics and the faithful are not permitted to participate in meetings, conferences or public celebrations during which the credibility of such "apparitions" would be taken for granted' — occasioned by seer Ivan Dragičević's scheduled appearances in US parishes, and reiterating Zadar as the operative judgment. A restriction on worship-adjacent events while the judgment was pending, not a ruling on the events. Cited from a labeled document mirror.

Holy See — the Hoser missions: 'an exclusively pastoral character' (not the apparitions) 11 February 2017 and 31 May 2018

◆Church act on a related matter — not a ruling on the apparition

Pope Francis, through the Secretariat of State: Archbishop Henryk Hoser as Special Envoy (2017), then as Apostolic Visitor of a special character for the parish, ad nutum Sanctae Sedis (2018).

Both acts say in their own text what they are not. The 2017 communiqué states the envoy's mission 'will therefore have an exclusively pastoral character'; the 2018 communiqué appoints the Visitor 'a tempo indeterminato e ad nutum Sanctae Sedis' for 'un incarico esclusivamente pastorale' — an exclusively pastoral office. These acts organize the care of pilgrims at a shrine; they presuppose the devotion and expressly exclude the question of the apparitions. That is why the rung is marked adjacent rather than favourable.

Holy See — official pilgrimages authorized (expressly not an authentication) 12 May 2019

◆Church act on a related matter — not a ruling on the apparition

Pope Francis; announced at Medjugorje by Nuncio Luigi Pezzuto and Archbishop Hoser; interim press office director Alessandro Gisotti supplied the official caveat.

Dioceses and parishes may now organize official pilgrimages to Medjugorje. The act's own frame, per Gisotti, is pastoral and explicitly non-adjudicating: care must be taken 'to prevent these pilgrimages from being interpreted as an authentication of known events, which still require examination by the Church', avoiding 'confusion or ambiguity from the doctrinal point of view'; the authorization 'is part of the particular pastoral attention that the Holy Father intended to give to that reality, aimed at encouraging and promoting the fruits of good.' No press-office bulletin exists for the day; the textual record is Vatican News.

DDF — nihil obstat, 'The Queen of Peace': devotion authorized, supernaturality expressly not declared 19 September 2024 (approved by Pope Francis 28 August 2024)

✓Investigated — concluded in favour

The Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith — Cardinal Víctor Manuel Fernández, Prefect, and Msgr. Armando Matteo, Secretary for the Doctrinal Section — under the Norms of 17 May 2024.

The Note 'The Queen of Peace' grants the nihil obstat, the ordinary favourable outcome under the 2024 Norms, and defines it in its own §38: the faithful 'are authorized to give it their adherence in a prudent manner… While this does not imply a declaration of the supernatural character of the phenomenon in question (cf. Norms, art. 22, §2)—and recalling that the faithful are not obliged to believe in it—the Nihil obstat indicates that the faithful can receive a positive encouragement for their Christian life through this spiritual proposal, and it authorizes public acts of devotion.' On the messages: 'the positive assessment that most of the messages of Medjugorje are edifying does not imply a declaration that they have a direct supernatural origin', so one should always speak of 'alleged messages'; 'some less important or unclear texts should be read with prudence'; future messages require the Apostolic Visitor's approval before publication, and the Note must be printed as the introduction to any collection. The object of this favourable act is the devotion and the spiritual experience around it. For the apparitions themselves the Norms' art. 23 states that, as a rule, no authority — bishop, conference or Dicastery — will declare such phenomena supernatural: on that object, the terminal status is stated in the document itself, by design.

About

Work-in-progress dataset compiled from public sources (see references). The apparitions are recorded here as REPORTED events: the dataset documents who reported what and when, and what Church authorities ruled and when — it does not assert the supernatural claim as fact, and neither, expressly, does the Church act most often read as doing so (the 2024 nihil obstat states it 'does not imply a declaration of the supernatural character of the phenomenon'). The documentary base is uneven and the dataset says where. The key pre-1998 Church texts — the Zadar Declaration of 1991, Bishop Žanić's 1990 statement, the 1987 communiqué, Cardinal Bertone's 1998 letter — have no primary institutional hosting online and are cited to labeled document mirrors; the Diocese of Mostar-Duvno's own site was unreachable from this session, so its texts also rest on mirrors and Catholic press. The Ruini commission's findings were never published: every vote figure carried here is a 2017 press leak, and is labeled as one. The seers' biographical details (births, marriages, residences, the dates their reported secrets completed) rest on devotional literature and sympathetic press, not civil or parish records. Pilgrim totals are the shrine's own registers or estimates, with no independent count. Single-source and devotional-only dates are flagged with a ? mark and a dateNote naming the problem. Contested characterizations are attributed to their authors. Corrections against primary sources are welcome.

What it is
A series of Marian apparitions reported since 24–25 June 1981 by six young people of the parish of Medjugorje (Diocese of Mostar-Duvno, Herzegovina — then Yugoslavia, now Bosnia and Herzegovina), attributed by the seers to the Virgin Mary under the title 'Queen of Peace' (Kraljica Mira). Three of the six report apparitions daily to this day, which makes Medjugorje the longest-running major reported apparition in the Church's modern experience.[27] [34]
The six seers (ages in 1981)
Vicka Ivanković (16), Mirjana Dragićević (16), Ivan Dragičević (16), Marija Pavlović (16), Ivanka Ivanković (15) and Jakov Čolo (10). Most were from the hamlet of Bijakovići below Podbrdo hill; Mirjana was from Sarajevo, summering with her grandmother. Sources conflict on Ivanka's birth date (21 June vs 21 July 1966), and the seers' life details generally rest on devotional literature.[27] [33]
The parish
St. James (Sv. Jakov), Medjugorje, Diocese of Mostar-Duvno — staffed then and now by Franciscans of the Herzegovinian province. That fact is central to the whole story: since Paul VI's 1975 decree Romanis Pontificibus ordered the friars to cede most Herzegovinian parishes to diocesan clergy, and many refused, the diocese and the province were already in open conflict when the reports began — the 'Herzegovina affair' against which critics read Medjugorje's rise.[27] [15] [40]
Reported ongoing apparitions (as of 2024)
Vicka, Ivan and Marija report daily apparitions; Mirjana reports one annually on 18 March, Ivanka annually on 25 June, Jakov annually at Christmas. Marija reports a public 'message to the world' every 25th of the month, published by the shrine. Mirjana's additional 2nd-of-the-month apparitions, reported from 1987, ended by her own announcement in March 2020. All of this is the seers' report, published by the shrine; no Church act authenticates any message's origin.[33] [39] [21] [1]
The 'ten secrets' (as reported)
In the reported structure, each seer is to receive ten secrets concerning future events, and a seer's daily apparitions cease when the tenth is received. Mirjana reported completing hers on 25 December 1982, Ivanka on 7 May 1985, Jakov on 12 September 1998; Vicka, Ivan and Marija report nine each. A promised visible 'sign' on Podbrdo hill is part of the reported structure. All dates in this scheme are seer-reported and carried by devotional literature.[33] [34]
Church status — the bottom line
No Church authority has ever declared the apparitions supernatural. The operative judgment from 1991 to 2024 was the Zadar Declaration of the Yugoslav bishops: 'On the basis of the investigations so far it can not be affirmed that one is dealing with supernatural apparitions and revelations' (non constat de supernaturalitate). On 19 September 2024 the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith granted a nihil obstat to devotion connected with Medjugorje — while stating in the same document that this 'does not imply a declaration of the supernatural character of the phenomenon'.[9] [17] [1]
Scale, with the counters named
The only hard series is self-reported: the shrine's own registers, as quoted by the DDF at its 19 September 2024 press conference, record 47.4 million Communions distributed between January 1985 and June 2024 and over a million concelebrating priests. Yearly pilgrim estimates diverge — about 1 million per year in web-based estimates cited at the same press conference, against about 3 million claimed by Archbishop Hoser before the pandemic — and the circulating '40+ million pilgrims since 1981' has no counting authority behind it. No independent count exists.[3] [34]
Who publishes the 'messages'
The official channel is the parish's Information Centre 'Mir' Medjugorje (medjugorje.hr), which publishes the monthly messages and the shrine statistics. The 2024 DDF Note instructs that the texts always be treated as 'alleged messages', requires the Apostolic Visitor's approval for the publication of any future ones, and orders the Note itself printed as the introduction to any collection of them. The widely-read sites medjugorje.com and medjugorje.org are unaffiliated devotional operations — medjugorje.com belongs to the contested 'Caritas of Birmingham' (see disambiguation).[39] [1] [35] [36]
Governance today
Since 2018 the parish has had an Apostolic Visitor of a special character, appointed ad nutum Sanctae Sedis (at the Holy See's pleasure) with 'an exclusively pastoral office': Archbishop Henryk Hoser from May 2018 until his death in August 2021, Archbishop Aldo Cavalli since November 2021. The office was created alongside, not in place of, the Diocese of Mostar-Duvno's ordinary jurisdiction.[6] [3]
What Medjugorje is NOT
Not an apparition 'approved as supernatural'. The 2024 nihil obstat certifies spiritual fruits and authorizes public devotion; the DDF's own Norms state (art. 23) that as a rule neither the diocesan bishop, nor the episcopal conferences, nor the Dicastery will declare such phenomena to be of supernatural origin. Headlines of the form 'Vatican approves Medjugorje apparitions' misreport the act.[1] [2]

Vicka Ivanković-Mijatović

b. 3 September 1964, Bijakovići · Bosnia and Herzegovina

Reported seer, the oldest of the six (16 in 1981); reports daily apparitions to this day and nine of the ten secrets. Married, two children, living at Krehin Gradac near Medjugorje — life details, like all the seers', from devotional literature and sympathetic press[33] [27]

Mirjana Dragićević-Soldo

b. 18 March 1965, Sarajevo · Bosnia and Herzegovina

Reported seer (16 in 1981), from Sarajevo; the first to report all ten secrets (25 December 1982). Reports an annual apparition each 18 March; her reported 2nd-of-the-month apparitions ran from 1987 until her 2020 announcement of their end. Married to Marko Soldo, two daughters, lives in Medjugorje[33] [21]

Ivan Dragičević

b. 25 May 1965, Bijakovići · Bosnia and Herzegovina / USA

Reported seer (16 in 1981); reports daily apparitions and nine secrets. Married American Laureen Murphy, four children; divides the year between the Boston area and Medjugorje — his US parish appearances occasioned the 2013 Viganò letter. Marriage and residence details from devotional biographies and sympathetic press, with no civil-record verification[33] [9]

Marija Pavlović-Lunetti

b. 1 April 1965, Bijakovići · Bosnia and Herzegovina / Italy

Reported seer (16 in 1981); reports daily apparitions, nine secrets, and the public 25th-of-the-month 'message to the world'. Donated a kidney to her brother in Alabama in 1988 — the visit behind the contested 'Caritas of Birmingham' connection. Married Paolo Lunetti of Monza (1993 or 1994, per devotional sources), four children, lives near Milan[33] [35]

Ivanka Ivanković-Elez

b. 1966 (day disputed), Bijakovići · Bosnia and Herzegovina

Reported seer (15 in 1981) — the first to report seeing the figure on 24 June 1981. Jakov Čolo

b. 6 March 1971, Bijakovići · Bosnia and Herzegovina

Reported seer, the youngest (10 in 1981); reported his tenth secret on 12 September 1998 and since then one annual apparition at Christmas. Lives in Medjugorje, married to an Italian, three children[33]

Fr. Jozo Zovko, OFM

b. 1941 · Bosnia and Herzegovina

Pastor of St. James in June 1981 — initially skeptical of the children, then their defender; arrested 17 August 1981, tried and imprisoned by the communist authorities (sentence reported as 3 or 3.5 years; released early), later a globally travelling Medjugorje preacher. His later canonical situation — reported faculty restrictions in the 1990s–2000s — is contested between devotional defenders and the Mostar chancery, and is left flagged here[27] [32]

Tomislav Vlašić

b. 1942 · Bosnia and Herzegovina / Italy

Franciscan assigned to Medjugorje in the early 1980s, widely described as the seers' first 'spiritual director' (a label whose formality the Mostar chancery and critics dispute); moved to Italy and founded the mixed community 'Kraljice mira'. Subject of the three Roman person-acts in this chronology: the 2008 CDF disciplinary decree, laicization at his own request in 2009, and the 2020 declaration of excommunication for simulated ministry. Fr. Slavko Barbarić, OFM

11 March 1946 – 24 November 2000 · Bosnia and Herzegovina

Herzegovinian Franciscan, doctorate in religious pedagogy; the seers' spiritual guide from the mid-1980s and the builder of Medjugorje's pastoral apparatus — fasting seminars, the Mother's Village orphanage. Bp. Pavao Žanić

1918–2000 · Bosnia and Herzegovina

Bishop of Mostar-Duvno 1980–1993. Publicly defended the seers' sincerity in 1981 ('the children are not lying'), then became the phenomenon's most vocal episcopal critic; convened the diocesan commissions (1982–1986) and published 'The Truth About Medjugorje' (1990) after jurisdiction had passed to the Bishops' Conference. His reversal has two competing explanations in the literature — police pressure (sympathetic sources) or the Ivica Vego affair and the taped early interviews (critical sources) — recorded as perspectives[13] [16] [17]

Bp. Ratko Perić

b. 1944 · Bosnia and Herzegovina

Bishop of Mostar-Duvno 1993–2020. Disambiguation & nuance

Medjugorje has one of the largest devotional literatures of any modern reported apparition, and the distinctions below are the ones most often flattened in it — starting with what the 2024 Roman act does and does not say.

The 2024 nihil obstat is not a declaration that Our Lady appeared

The DDF's Note 'The Queen of Peace' says so itself, in the sentence that grants the favour: through the nihil obstat the faithful 'are authorized to give it their adherence in a prudent manner… While this does not imply a declaration of the supernatural character of the phenomenon in question (cf. Norms, art. 22, §2)—and recalling that the faithful are not obliged to believe in it—the Nihil obstat indicates that the faithful can receive a positive encouragement for their Christian life through this spiritual proposal, and it authorizes public acts of devotion.' That is not a hedge added by this page; it is the act's own definition of itself. The underlying rule is the Norms of 17 May 2024, art. 23: as a rule, neither the diocesan bishop, nor the episcopal conferences, nor the Dicastery will declare that these phenomena are of supernatural origin. What the 2024 act judges favourably is the devotion and its fruits; the apparitions' supernatural character is the question the Church has now said it does not, as a rule, answer. Headlines of the form 'Vatican approves the Medjugorje apparitions' misreport the document they cite.[1] [2]

Caritas of Birmingham and medjugorje.com are not the Medjugorje shrine

'Caritas of Birmingham' (Sterrett, Alabama, founded 1986 by Terry Colafrancesco, 'A Friend of Medjugorje') is not affiliated with the Medjugorje parish or shrine, with the Catholic Church, or with the Caritas Internationalis confederation whose name it echoes. Its site medjugorje.com — one of the highest-traffic Medjugorje addresses — is not an official Medjugorje site; the official channel is the parish's Information Centre 'Mir' at medjugorje.hr, and the US 'Medjugorje Web' at medjugorje.org is likewise an unaffiliated devotional operation. The Alabama organization's record — the local bishop's bar on priests celebrating Mass there, ex-members' lawsuits and cult allegations, settled privately — is documented in secular reporting, and warning dossiers on it are maintained even by the sympathetic devotional site medjugorje.ws. An organization's domain name is not a chain of custody.[35] [36] [39]

'Messages' means 'alleged messages' — by Rome's own instruction

The monthly 'messages to the world' and the older message series are the seers' reports, published by the shrine; no Church act authenticates their origin. The 2024 Note makes the required framing explicit: 'the positive assessment that most of the messages of Medjugorje are edifying does not imply a declaration that they have a direct supernatural origin. Consequently, when referring to "messages" from Our Lady, one should always bear in mind that they are "alleged messages"'; 'some less important or unclear texts should be read with prudence'. The same Note requires the Apostolic Visitor's approval for the publication of any future messages, and orders the Note itself printed as the introduction to any collection of them. This dataset follows that usage throughout: every message is a reported message.[1]

The acts about Tomislav Vlašić judge a man, not the apparitions

Three Roman acts concern Vlašić: the CDF's disciplinary decree of 25 January 2008 (dubious doctrine, manipulation of consciences, suspected mysticism, disobedience, acts contra sextum, suspicion of heresy and schism), his laicization at his own request in 2009, and the 2020 declaration of excommunication for continuing to simulate priestly ministry. They are acts about his doctrine and conduct. They are routinely cited in polemics as if they were a Roman verdict on Medjugorje — in both directions: as discrediting the apparitions (he was the seers' early 'spiritual director', a label whose formality is itself disputed), or as needing to be minimized to protect them. The dataset keeps the object of each act named: a person's case is not an apparition's case, in either direction.[11] [26] [22]

The bishops of Mostar's 'no' is documented — and so is the Holy See's classification of it

Bishops Žanić (1990) and Perić (1997, 2006, 2017) published unambiguous negative judgments — Perić going to constat de non supernaturalitate, 'the Madonna has not appeared in Medjugorje'. The CDF's 1998 Bertone letter states that the Mostar bishop's declaration 'is and remains his personal opinion', the operative judgment being the Yugoslav bishops' 1991 non constat; Perić's 2017 text presents the negative judgment as 'the position of this Curia throughout this period'. Devotional apologetics quotes the CDF's classification to dismiss the bishops; critical literature quotes the bishops as if they were the Church's verdict. Both moves flatten a documented, unresolved tension. This dataset carries both texts, attributed, and resolves nothing.[9] [10] [19]

24 June or 25 June 1981 — both dates are 'the first apparition', about different things

24 June is the first reported sighting (two, then four, young people); 25 June is the first reported apparition to the group of six together, and the date the shrine keeps as the official anniversary. Both dates appear as 'the' date in different sources, and the difference is not an error in either — it is two events. The chronology carries both explicitly.[27] [34]

The Ruini votes are a leak, not a published finding

The figures everyone quotes — 13 votes to 1 (with 1 suspensive) in favour of the supernatural character of the first seven appearances, doubts about everything after — come from Andrea Tornielli's report of 16 May 2017, not from any published document. The commission's constitution (2010) and the delivery of its findings (January 2014) are documented; the findings themselves have never been published, and no press bulletin exists even for the delivery. Pope Francis's 'the Ruini report is very, very good' three days before the leak is the nearest thing to official corroboration, and it is a spoken remark. Wherever this dataset mentions the votes, it labels them leak-reported.[18] [4] [12] [7]
